**Capstone Project Guideline:  
Online Retail Customer Analytics & Segmentation**

**1\. Robust Data Engineering Workflow**

Clean data guarantees reliable business logic. Document every decision with its business impact.

**Structural Cleansing & Type Casting**

- **Temporal Formatting:** Convert InvoiceDate to a datetime object. Extract Year, Month, DayOfWeek, and Hour for seasonal patterns.
- **Missing Values:** Do not blindly drop rows missing CustomerID. Group them by InvoiceNo to check for guest checkout blocks before isolating them.

**Transactional Validation & Anomalies**

- **Cancellations:** Invoices starting with 'C' indicate returns and carry negative quantities. Match them with original invoices where possible to net out revenue.
- **Zero/Negative Filters:** Isolate transactions with UnitPrice = 0 or negative values as they represent adjustments, system errors, or company giveaways.

**Feature Generation**

- **Line Revenue:** Create TotalLineRevenue = Quantity \* UnitPrice.
- **Geographic Binary:** Since the dataset is heavily UK-focused, create an Is_UK flag to isolate international trends from local skew.

**2\. Strategic Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)**

Transform graphs into business narratives addressing executive-level questions.

Do not begin with clustering. First decide what the business needs to understand.

- Which products generate the most units sold and the most revenue?
- Which customers contribute the most revenue and orders?
- How does revenue change over time?
- Which months, weekdays, and hours are strongest?
- Which countries generate the most customers, orders, and revenue?
- Are there seasonal purchasing patterns?
- How common are cancellations and returns, and which products/customers are associated with them?
- What does a typical customer's purchasing behavior look like?
- Are there signs of wholesale/bulk-buying behavior?
- Can customers be grouped into actionable segments based on their value and engagement?

**Analysis Framework**

- **Macro-Level Trends:** Plot monthly revenue to evaluate Q4 holiday surges. Analyze hourly traffic to optimize server maintenance and ad-spend targeting.
- **Micro-Level Behavior:** Calculate Average Order Value (AOV) and basket sizes per invoice to determine wholesale vs. individual purchasing behaviors.
- **Product Concentration (Pareto Principle):** Verify if 80% of revenue comes from 20% of products by plotting a cumulative revenue curve of StockCodes.
- **Dead Stock Identification:** Isolate products with high historical volume but zero or negative sales over the final 90 days.

**3\. The Mechanics of RFM Analysis**

RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) translates historical transactions into three clear dimensions:

- **Recency (R):** Days since the customer's last purchase transaction.
- **Frequency (F):** Total number of unique invoices associated with a customer.
- **Monetary (M):** Sum of all transaction revenues generated by a customer.

**Market Importance**

- **Actionable Personalization:** Prevents over-discounting loyal clients while targeting aggressive retention offers precisely to churning users.
- **Churn Prediction:** Provides an early warning indicator through falling Recency scores before an account is permanently lost.
- **Maximizing Lifetime Value:** Systematically separates high-value corporate wholesalers from small-batch buyers to protect core revenue lines.

**Scoring Setup**

Divide metrics into 5 equal quantiles using pd.qcut, scoring 1 to 5. For Recency, lower days since last purchase must equal a higher score (5 = most recent). Combine scores into a string cell (e.g., '555' represents a top-tier customer).

**4\. Algorithmic Customer Segmentation**

**Preprocessing Pre-requisites**

- **Skewness Transformation:** Apply a Log-Transformation to compress heavy right-hand skews driven by wholesale volumes.
- **Standardization:** Scale columns with StandardScaler to a mean of 0 and variance of 1. Unscaled monetary figures will dwarf recency distances in Euclidean space.

**Clustering Comparison**

| Algorithm    | Strengths / Weaknesses                                                                             | Best Applied To                                            |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| K-Means      | Efficient, highly interpretable / Sensitive to outliers, assumes spherical clusters                | Baseline segmentation when clusters are separated cleanly. |
| Hierarchical | No fixed cluster count needed, visible dendrogram / Computationally expensive on large data arrays | Sampling sub-cohorts to find optimal tree heights.         |
| DBSCAN       | Isolates extreme outliers (whale buyers), arbitrary shapes / Struggles with varying density levels | Filtering out data noise or anomalous massive orders.      |

**Quantitative Validation**

- **Elbow Method:** Locate the inflection point where Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS) drop-off begins to plateau.
- **Silhouette Width:** Measure cluster separation boundaries to maximize separation without overlapping assignments.

**5\. Business Translation Matrix**

Map abstract cluster numbers directly into strategic corporate marketing actions:

- **Champions (R=5, F=5, M=5):** Most profitable buyers. Strategy: Reward with loyalty perks, exclusive early access, and brand referral programs.
- **Loyal Customers (R=4, F=4, M=4):** Consistent regular spenders. Strategy: Maximize revenue via cross-selling related product catalog segments.
- **At-Risk / High-Value Churn (Low R, High F, High M):** Heavy historic spenders who stopped buying. Strategy: Deploy immediate direct win-back email offers or severe promotional price triggers.
- **New Inflow (High R, Low F, Low M):** Recent first-time purchasers. Strategy: Onboard via welcome discount sequences to trigger a secondary purchase.

**6\. Deliverables Best Practices**

# 2\. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Starting with K-Means before understanding the business.
- Deleting negative quantities without explaining returns/cancellations.
- Treating every row as an order instead of a transaction line.
- Using CustomerID as a numerical feature.
- Clustering unscaled RFM variables.
- Using RFM scores without explaining their direction and meaning.
- Choosing K only because the elbow plot 'looks right'.
- Calling clusters 'VIP' or 'bad customers' without supporting statistics.
- Showing 20 charts but providing no business interpretation.
- Making causal claims from descriptive data.
- Copying a standard RFM segmentation and forcing the data to fit it.
- Submitting code that cannot be reproduced.

# 3\. What Excellent Work Looks Like

A strong project tells one coherent story: the students understand the retail business, establish trustworthy data, uncover patterns with evidence, translate transactions into customer behavior, use RFM to explain customer value and engagement, build defensible segments, and finish with specific business actions. The objective is not to maximize the number of algorithms or charts; it is to produce a clear, reproducible, evidence-based business analysis.
